# Product Brief — Medieval Persistent World RPG

**Stage:** Concept exploration, November 2026. All labels and world names provisional.

## Thesis

Torn demonstrates long-term appeal for persistent character advancement, social competition, trading, guild/faction identity, and a shared economy. A medieval fantasy setting offers analogous systems (swords, alchemy, guilds, trade routes, land ownership) and unique opportunities (resource geography, kingdoms, sieges, diplomacy). Autonomous NPC populations could reduce the initial empty-world problem and provide a useful simulation/testing substrate.

This is a *hypothesis*, not a validated market gap. Existing fantasy text MMOs and MUDs are competitors and reference points.

## Player fantasy

Start as an ordinary resident of an existing kingdom. Choose a livelihood. Become capable, wealthy, feared, respected, or politically influential. Rivalries and alliances evolve over time. Actions leave persistent traces in towns, markets, and institutions.

## Three pillars

1. **Persistent progression:** skills, occupations, equipment, reputation, long-term goals; diminish runaway power advantages without erasing seniority.
2. **Interdependent economy:** food, timber, iron, crafting, trade, caravans, regional supply/demand. No unrestricted NPC resource creation.
3. **Consequential social world:** guilds, rivalries, leaders, diplomacy, territorial competition; NPCs fill gaps but humans remain the protagonists.

## Player loops to prototype

- Short sessions: collect production, train a skill, take a contract, trade, respond to an encounter.
- Medium term: specialize, acquire assets, cultivate relationships, join a guild.
- Long term: build an enterprise, shape regional markets, compete for territory or political authority.

## Important unresolved design questions

- **Game-first vs. simulation-first:** how much autonomy should the world have when it conflicts with fun?
- **Session rhythm:** primarily asynchronous menu play, or more real-time events?
- **Power curve:** what lets a player joining in year five matter in week one?
- **NPC autonomy:** who may own assets, command guilds, or declare wars? Who can override NPC mistakes?
- **Economy:** resource sinks, money supply, taxes, welfare, NPC production/consumption; how prevent spirals and monopolies?
- **Territory:** single shared world with no shards, or regional boundaries; safeguards against one dominant alliance?
- **Disclosure:** label NPC-controlled entities clearly, including in chat.
- **Monetization:** subscriptions/cosmetics/convenience; avoid selling unfair competitive advantages.
- **Accessibility:** excellent mobile browser UX, fast interactions, text readability and screen reader support.

## Early validation questions

Would 20 real users interacting with 200 simulated residents return over multiple days? Can NPCs make the market useful without making human trading pointless? Can users tell what consequences their actions caused? Are economic outcomes comprehensible? Do emergent rivalries remain enjoyable rather than punitive?

**North-star for the experiment:** repeated *meaningful human actions*, not aggregate NPC activity.
